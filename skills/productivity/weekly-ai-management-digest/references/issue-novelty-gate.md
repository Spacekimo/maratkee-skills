# Issue Novelty and Portfolio Gate

Run this gate before source collection and outlining every recurring issue. Its purpose is continuity without repeatedly proving the same management conclusion.

## 1. Build the issue ledger

Inspect at least the four preceding issues. For each, record:

- title and one-sentence thesis;
- `primary_lane`: organisation design; people and professional growth; portfolio and economics; or practice and reliability;
- reader decision;
- dominant motifs: the ideas that appear in the title, lead, first section, action list or final question.

This is an editorial record, not a keyword count. A fresh source or a different example does not make a familiar conclusion new.

## 2. Enforce lane rotation

Give the candidate one `primary_lane` and an optional `secondary_lane`.

A primary lane cannot be reused as primary for the next three issues. A secondary mention does not reset the rotation. If a candidate cannot be assigned a clear lane, it is not ready for research.

The four lanes are:

1. **Organisation design** — team boundaries, role interfaces, decision rights and operating-model changes.
2. **People and professional growth** — skills, career paths, mentoring, identity and leadership work.
3. **Portfolio and economics** — capacity, investment, demand, value, trade-offs and business outcomes.
4. **Practice and reliability** — operating an AI-enabled workflow or system: quality, safety, observability, cost and repeatability.

## 3. Quarantine dominant motifs

A motif becomes quarantined for four subsequent issues when it was the title, lead, dominant first section, action list and final question of a published issue.

During quarantine it may appear only as a one- or two-sentence bridge or as an acceptance criterion. It cannot set the candidate's title, opening, main action list or recommended-reading spine.

Examples of motifs that can require quarantine after use:

- local acceleration versus end-to-end flow;
- review as the bottleneck;
- permissioning, control routes and escalation;
- harnesses, sandboxes, traces and other operating controls;
- run cost or token economics;
- delegation contracts.

Use the ledger to define the actual quarantine list. Do not apply the examples mechanically.

## 4. State the new management decision

Before full research, complete this sentence:

> This issue helps a manager make or improve **[specific decision]** about **[specific object]** using **[new evidence or mechanism]**.

The decision, object or mechanism must materially differ from the preceding issues and must not rely on a quarantined motif. A strong incident can open the issue, provided it leads to this new decision rather than recreating a familiar technical-controls article.

## 5. Classify planned material

For every planned section and action item, mark it:

- **new core** — it introduces the distinct decision, object, mechanism or evidence of this issue;
- **bridge** — it connects to a prior issue in one or two sentences;
- **repeat** — it restates a prior conclusion without creating a new decision, mechanism or evidence.

Remove `repeat` material. Keep bridges short. At least 75% of planned visible prose and every action item must be `new core`.

## 6. Test the whole editorial promise

The title, description, opening, section order, actions, final question and reading list must make the new management tension visible. Changing only body paragraphs leaves the former editorial promise in place.

If the candidate fails this test, change the issue object before deep research. Do not try to rescue it with a fresher source, a new metaphor or a title swap.

## 7. Record the decision

Add an `issue-novelty` block to the editorial review artifact containing:

- the four-issue ledger;
- chosen primary and secondary lanes;
- active quarantined motifs;
- the new management decision;
- retained bridge material;
- repeated material removed;
- the final verdict: `pass`, `reframe`, or `hold`.
